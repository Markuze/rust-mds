## 2. E2: structural completion is not grant release

Keep these signatures. Add only the unit input shown here:

```rust
fn finish_reconnect(&mut self, keys: &[SessionKey]) -> Result<EngineStep, RestoreError>;
// Additional EngineInput variant:
RecoveryControlsDrained,
fn step(&mut self, input: Option<EngineInput>, permit: Option<RecordPermit>)
    -> Result<EngineStep, StepError>;
```

Use three private runtime phases. They are not WAL/checkpoint fields or new monitor states:

| Phase | Allowed work | Prohibited work |
|---|---|---|
| Reconstruction | Validated chunks; fenced Closing/cleanup/Closed; journal completion | Requests, cap-update/release admission, new cap grants, data-job execution, new stray purge |
| Control drain | CapUpdate, CapRelease, CloseSession, Tick, LifecycleCompleted, monotonic revokes and eligible retained jobs | Requests, new cap grants, new unclaimed-stray purge |
| Grants released | Normal engine stepping, including committed-retry reconstruction | Ordinary daemon admission until recovery output drains and ACTIVE is confirmed |

Every checked restore starts in Reconstruction, including a checkpoint just written by fresh Ready bootstrap. Only a direct, non-restored bootstrap constructor can retain its serving initialization; that exception does not describe the production load path. Lost authority or engine failure closes output and work permissions in every phase.

### Structural completion

`finish_reconnect(keys)` requires a duplicate-free set equal to **all surviving Open keys**, not the empty missing-member set. Every key must have a validated final chunk. Every excluded key must have completed fenced cleanup and durable Closed before the daemon calls this method. Require no pending commit or continuation and submitted = durable = journal committed. Invalid state returns `RestoreError::Invalid`; a failed engine returns `RestoreError::Failed`. Neither error changes phase or emits output.

Success enters Control drain. It submits no record, takes no permit and returns no unused permit. It may schedule bounded revoke evaluation and expose eligible retained lifecycle intents through the normal effects/query interface. It must not issue grants, build grant payloads, or queue new stray purge. Return the normal runnable step state. `Idle` means no current runnable work, not that grants are released.

Retain the accepted key set for this recovery attempt. A repeated call with that same duplicate-free set is a no-op in either later phase. Return current step state with empty effects/notices; do not evaluate caps or re-emit jobs. A different set is invalid. A later session close does not make this duplicate call a new reconstruction operation. Chunks and `reset_reconnect` cannot reopen structural reconstruction after success. Production must not use legacy `reconnect_done` to bypass either boundary.

### Suppression and progress

The grant gate applies from restore until release, including missing-session cleanup **before** structural completion. Cover every path: cap evaluation, session resume, reply caps, range sharing, cleanup and journal callbacks. Do not advance grant state/sequences and then hide the output. Retain deferred evaluation identities, not prebuilt grants. Retain inode/client identities for deferred max-size notifications too; deliver the committed range after release even if the cap mask did not change. Recompute grant payloads from committed state after release.

Allow revokes that only remove already effective rights. Do not add rights or increase advertised `max_size` in a revoke, TRUNC or acknowledgment. Defer a stale-cap reissue that would restore effective rights. Preserve cap IDs, sequence rules, committed TRUNC notifications, FLUSH_ACK and terminal cleanup notices. A new dirty flush receives FLUSH_ACK only after its record commits. A history-proven duplicate may be acknowledged again without applying its metadata.

After structural completion, `pending_jobs()` and lifecycle completion must not require grant release or ACTIVE. Keep their existing eligibility checks. RecoverSize requires no issued writer rights and no conflicting truncate. Revoke those rights during Control drain; process their acknowledgments. Keep lost-writer bounds and job inputs until completion commits.

For a dirty CapUpdate blocked by RecoverSize with no issued writer rights, return `Busy` without consuming or changing the input/permit. The daemon retains that control and runs other eligible revoke acknowledgments, truncate/probe work and committed job completion **ahead of retrying that blocked control**. It must not put those dependencies behind that control in one strict FIFO. If the sender still has issued writer rights, admit its dirty revoke acknowledgment under the existing rules. In the same flush record, supersede each affected probe guard with a fresh job ID, the new inode version/truncate sequence, all prior layouts and at least the prior maximum bound. Existing lifecycle-intent/retirement fields already support this. Ignore old job results. Do not weaken probe guards or fabricate an empty-file result to make progress.

Deferred grant-only evaluation is not runnable admission work during Control drain. Do not spin `step(None, ...)` on it. Preserve runnable revokes, controls and job completions separately from that wait.

### Explicit release

The daemon serializes `RecoveryControlsDrained` behind its ordered recovery-control cut. Include early controls sent before final chunks, all eligible controls already received at the cut, durable attachments, missing-key cleanup, and jobs required to drain those controls. Each dirty control must be committed/deduplicated, or its key must complete fenced durable cleanup. A transport reset or deleted queue entry is not completion. The daemon must account for retained input outside the engine. Held ordinary requests do not block this cut.

- In Reconstruction, or with a pending commit/earlier continuation, `step(Some(RecoveryControlsDrained), permit)` returns `StepError::Busy { input, permit }` unchanged. No release occurs. Wait for the relevant completion; do not spin.
- In Control drain with no earlier engine work, a call without a permit retains the marker as the normal continuation, returns `NeedsAdmission`, and keeps grants closed. `step(None, Some(permit))` executes it. Do not submit the marker again while the engine owns it.
- On execution, release the gate once and schedule bounded fresh evaluation. The marker itself submits no record. Return the permit unused unless that step performs one permitted follow-on transaction. Use ordinary `NeedsAdmission`, `WaitingCommit(seq)` and `WaitingInput` for remaining work; materialize each effect vector before another admitted step.
- A duplicate marker in Grants released is a no-op with empty effects/notices and the permit returned. Pending-commit/continuation exclusion still applies. No new notice or schema field is needed. Engine failure keeps the existing terminal `Failed` behavior.

An empty gather/control cut takes the same two completion boundaries. Grant release does not itself admit ordinary requests. If recovery replies/writes remain after confirmed REJOIN, request and confirm CLIENTREPLAY, then drain known committed retries, required jobs/writes and ordered recovery output through each session funnel's transport-acceptance boundary. This requires no new client acknowledgment. An empty cut still executes the internal hook/marker, but needs no monitor-visible CLIENTREPLAY. Then request and confirm ACTIVE. Replay startup must retain monitor-visible REJOIN.

## 3. E1: committed creator replies through Request/Reply

Use `EngineInput::Request` and `Effect::Reply`. A completion hit requires the same durable session incarnation, tid and **original operation**, before history trim or embedded releases. Check the table before new execution even if the retry counter is zero; an in-flight duplicate also cannot start a second transaction. Preserve the request's validation context and original wire op/tid; never rewrite the reply op to LOOKUP/GETATTR. A same-tid/different-op input is a protocol-identity violation, not a new operation or a completed hit. Reject it through daemon validation without mutation, history trim or an invented reply from the other operation. Do not forward an answered tid. This does not assert that an empty `-EINVAL` is safe for every malformed client state.

Keep durable `op/result/created_ino` unchanged. Use recorded result, not literal zero. The ordinary changed-identity error below is a **wire/application error**, not a changed durable outcome. Strip obsolete embedded releases on recovery retries. Do not manufacture REPLAY to obtain a shortcut. Supported completions record successful mutations; this addendum does not add failed-completion records.

### 3.1 Matching wire REPLAY: safe completion only (R1)

REPLAY means the client already received an unsafe result. It is **not** a synonym for an ordinary retry after a lost safe-only reply. For a matching committed REPLAY, send `safe=true`, the original op/tid and **recorded result**, with **empty target/dentry trace and empty snap trace**, regardless of subsequent rename, replacement or purge. Do not reopen, reconstruct a trace, issue caps/ranges, or substitute a changed-path error. For CREATE with recorded `Some(I)`, preserve `openc_response_t { created_ino: I, delegated_inos: empty }`; non-CREATE operations carry no CREATE extra. This keeps extra encoding valid for the original opcode, rather than copying C++'s non-CREATE duplicate-extra quirk.

Proof: `Client::send_request` sets REPLAY only for `got_unsafe` (`client/Client.cc:2852–2855`). `handle_client_reply` calls `insert_trace` before safe completion (`:3060–3062`), and `insert_trace` asserts an empty trace for `got_unsafe` **before** its ordinary empty-trace branch (`:1781–1785`). Kernel `mds_client.c:3540–3574,4117–4138` has the same unsafe/replay distinction and does not revise an already-delivered result on safe completion. C++ `Server::handle_client_request` has the separate traceless shortcut (`mds/Server.cc:2657–2684`). Safe-only Rust normally creates no genuine unsafe-reply state, but must not send a traced reply to this wire branch.

### 3.2 Ordinary retry with no earlier reply: case matrix

**ACCEPTED E1 contract; implementation remains separate.** The user selected the Clean-session boundary on **2026-10-25**. This replaces the former unresolved owner-choice rows. Enable this rule only after sections 3.5–3.8. Do not apply it retroactively to older Open histories.

Here `I = completion.reply_target_ino`, not necessarily `created_ino`. `P/n` is the **requested** parent/name. `D(P,n)` is a current parent/name trace. `T(I)` is a current committed target trace. Retention makes `I` resident even when it has no links or client caps.

| Ordinary retry state | Result and required trace | Extra/limit |
|---|---|---|
| Named CREATE/MKDIR/MKNOD/SYMLINK, or mutating named OPEN; `P/n -> I` | Recorded success; valid requested `D(P,n) + T(I)` | A requested hard link is valid when another link is primary. An ancestor rename is harmless when the same parent remains the request base. |
| Original OPEN directly names `I` and needs no dentry | Recorded success; `T(I)` | Never turn a named creator into this case. |
| Old name absent; `I` moved or unlinked | **SAFE `-EINTR`; CAPLESS `T(I)` only**, no dentry, empty snap trace | Uniform policy, not null-dentry `-ENOENT`. No old link is asserted. |
| Requested name now names `J != I` | Same **CAPLESS original `T(I)` / `-EINTR`** | No target `J`, false null dentry or false positive old link. |
| Parent/path no longer resolves at the requested parent | Same **CAPLESS original `T(I)` / `-EINTR`** | No new-primary-parent substitution. Includes retained unlinked directory and symlink targets. |
| `created_ino=None`, but mutating CREATE/OPEN has `reply_target_ino=Some(I)` | Apply the same unchanged/changed identity rules | Do not fabricate creation or a created-inode extra. |
| Older valid Open/Closing histories, including missing `I` or absent target field | **`UpgradeRequired`**, before service or adoption | Do not classify a valid legacy image as `Corrupt`; do not guess `I` or serve an empty-error workaround. |
| Semantics-3 affected completion lacks `I`, or a retained `I` is missing | Reject checked state as an invariant violation | This is damage to a format that promises retention, not the legacy case. No client output. |

CREATE carries `openc_response_t { created_ino: original_created_ino, delegated_inos: empty }` **only when its completion records creation**. Other opcodes carry no CREATE extra. `created_ino=None` remains None and adds no created-inode extra, even when `reply_target_ino=Some(I)`. Preserve original op/tid and the successful durable result on every branch. Other non-open-like completed mutations retain their recorded-result deduplication rule.

Reconstruction never repeats allocation, link insertion, O_EXCL/O_TRUNC, initial xattrs, timestamps or the completion transaction. Unchanged success may reconstruct normal caps from committed state. If CREATE/OPEN success needs a fresh durable writer range, commit it in a separate admitted range transaction before output. It does not replace the original completion or create another creation. The changed-identity error performs **no** range transaction, cap creation/evaluation, cap sequence advance or data work.

CAPLESS means caps/wanted/cap-id/seq/mseq/flags are zero. Build current committed inode metadata with `inode_stat(..., issue=false)` or its private equivalent. Do not route the error through today's unconditional issue=true target builder. Set advertised `max_size=0`; do not alter a durable range. Include the actual symlink, layout, attributes and stable subvolume identity. Keep the normal directory wire convention `nlink=1` and `inline_version!=0`, even when the durable directory has zero links. Supply no parent/name, snap grants or historical namespace assertion. A private `ReplySpec::issue_caps: bool` is sufficient; set it false on this path. No new public reply type or general reply cache is needed.

Source proof is `current/astra-creator-retry-proof.md` sections 3–4 and 6 (especially row 59 and test 208). In the inspected C++ tree, `Client::insert_trace` consumes a **nonempty** target-only trace without dereferencing the old dentry directory (`client/Client.cc:1848–1868,1944–1973`). `add_update_inode` accepts zero caps (`:1244–1245`). `make_request` skips success verification for a negative result (`:2378–2403`). The creator callers return EINTR before successful-open/creator follow-up. Linux `handle_reply`/`ceph_fill_trace` insert target metadata with fmode -1 on error, without a dentry splice or a zero-cap grant (`fs/ceph/mds_client.c:4152–4187,4218–4229`; `inode.c:1063–1069,1710–1743`). EINTR takes the terminal error exit (`file.c:978–1005`; `dir.c:977–988,1097–1106,1177–1195`). These are behavior facts, not GPL translation or installed-peer qualification.

The uniform EINTR choice is application policy. It avoids kernel CREATE's special ENOENT/VFS follow-up (`file.c:957–983`). It is not a claim that C++ always sends EINTR: completed C++ CREATE can instead open current `J` with old create extra `I` (`mds/Server.cc:2896–2905,4660,4738,4818–4826`), which this contract forbids. An **empty** ordinary error is still not proved safe for a detached libcephfs request dentry. Retention supplies the real nonempty target instead. A negative ordinary reply is an application-visible failure after a committed mutation, not a special successful-reconstruction status. A new application request has a new tid and remains a separate operation. `check_invariants_for` is required but does not prove these identity semantics.

Add only the original-target identity to durable completions:

| Successful transaction | `created_ino` | `reply_target_ino` | Completion placement |
|---|---|---|---|
| New-inode CREATE, MKDIR, MKNOD, SYMLINK | `Some(I)` | `Some(I)` | Same record as allocation, inode and link; both identities must agree. |

### 3.4 Retention, durable retirement and bounds

For every durable semantics-3 successful creator or mutating CREATE/OPEN completion with `reply_target_ino=Some(I)`, retain the **current authoritative inode metadata** for `I`. This retains neither a historical dentry nor a serialized reply. Reconstruct references from canonical durable completions after restore and every replay transition, before purge eligibility or grant release. Multiple completions share one inode. Client caps, reconnect knowledge and current links are not prerequisites for a server history reference.

Stage the dependency with its inode/completion transaction. Existing pending holds and global commit serialization protect it before the callback. At commit, install the canonical completion/reference before releasing deferred output or evaluating purge/caps. If the append is durable but the callback is lost, checked replay derives that same reference from the record. A sent error can be lost again; sending it does not retire its dependency.

Use the existing `Session::completed_requests` as the canonical **durable** table. Add a runtime `durable_oldest_client_tid`; keep `oldest_client_tid` as the observed volatile floor. A read-only input may advance the latter but cannot erase canonical entries or release their references. Check a completion hit first. Do not add a second independently trimmed retention table. A private reference-count index is only a derived accelerator and is rebuilt/checked against the canonical table.

Retire a completion only when either:

1. A **new completion-bearing WAL transaction commits** `CompletedRequest.oldest_client_tid` above the older completion's tid. Use `max(previous durable floor, recorded watermark)`. The new completion must satisfy `oldest_client_tid <= tid`; reject an invalid new request before changing state. Apply the identical trim/insertion rule at commit and during replay. Never advance this durable floor at submission, on an unknown append outcome, on a read-only input or merely from an observed volatile floor. A matching retry does not replace its completion to publish a watermark.
2. The existing terminal `closed_sessions: [SessionKey]` cleanup record commits. Rank Closing alone, or removal of runtime caps during earlier cleanup steps, is insufficient. A replacement incarnation cannot inherit the old table.

For RetainedTargets3, `CheckpointSession.oldest_client_tid` stores the **durable** floor. Export the entire canonical history above that floor, including entries hidden by a newer volatile floor. Restore initializes both runtime floors to that durable value. `checkpoint()`/`state_image`, publication, WAL trim and `RecoveryControlsDrained` release **no** additional references. No checkpoint-publication callback is added. A crash before watermark commit restores the older completion plus its target; a crash after it may correctly restore neither reference. Keep an admitted reply/range context's temporary inode hold until its target metadata has been materialized. Already-built effects own their metadata and use the daemon's existing bounded output budget.

Enforce the reference check in the final `purge_inode` decision **and** each eligibility path: immediate last-link removal in `unlink_dentry`, rename-overwrite/RMDIR, `maybe_purge_stray`, queued/unclaimed strays after reconnect, last-cap release, session cleanup, commit callbacks and lifecycle scheduling. A referenced inode remains a stray with its journaled authorization path, stable subvolume identity, layouts, symlink and attributes. Namespace/statistics/quota removal still occurs once at unlink. Postpone its existing **data purge intent together with metadata purge**; do not create a separate metadata-only/data-GC mechanism. Truncate/RecoverSize keep their existing independent guards and can progress. A semantics-3 Purge intent cannot coexist with a retained target. Checked replay validates the candidate **after** applying the record's durable trim/terminal retirement, and rejects a purge that leaves a surviving reference. The first implementation must not combine reference retirement and purge of that target in one new record: commit retirement, then admit ordinary purge separately. Recheck all remaining references and reply holds at that later turn.

Add `RetryLimits { max_completions: usize, max_bytes: usize }` and `EngineConfig::retry_limits`, with defaults `max_completions=65_536` and `max_bytes=64 MiB`. These bound canonical request completions **including non-target completions**, the derived reference index, and metadata of **all distinct referenced targets**, not just already-unlinked targets. Count a shared target once. Recompute the projected charge before any request mutation, embedded release, cap grant or new reference, and before any operation grows a referenced inode. Recovered semantics-3 state above configured limits returns Capacity without deleting state. Limits may be raised only by a reviewed same-identity restart; do not silently evict or expire histories.

Reserve projected charge/slots before accepting new **ordinary Request** work. Include reservations for parked pre-mutation requests in the base limit; another ordinary request cannot spend them. Revalidate before mutation. A pressure-blocked ordinary continuation remains owned under the input bound, is non-runnable and waits for an actual capacity-change event. Do not leave it as an exclusive runnable continuation that prevents fitting retirement work from passing. Ordinary pre-mutation reservations are revocable for a fitting protected control: return the affected requests to pressure wait and revalidate them later, without discarding their inputs or changing durable state. They are not persistent headroom for already-issued variable-metadata rights. The extra transient allowance below belongs only to the one active transaction/materialization, not to every parked request. Non-request controls use the terminal R4 rule below, not this ordinary wait rule.

Use this deterministic metadata charge: 256 bytes per completion; 4096 fixed bytes per distinct referenced inode; plus the recursively charged variable storage for its live and committed core copies, entries, parent and remote links. Charge byte buffers by capacity. Charge Vec storage as `capacity * max(size_of::<element>(), 256)` plus nested buffer charges. Charge each BTreeMap/Set entry as `256 + size_of::<key/value>()` plus nested buffer charges. Include every xattr key/value, symlink, fscrypt buffer, layout namespace, old layout/pool list, writer-range entry, dentry name and authorization path. Include any extra reference-index allocation in the same budget. Fixed charges cover scalar/node bookkeeping, not caps/locks/transport queues. This is an exact **accounting limit**, not a process RSS claim; the existing runtime/control/transport limits remain separate.

Committed charge must stay within 64 MiB and 65,536 entries. Reserve one extra completion slot and **8 MiB** of transient charge for the single pending transaction/reply context; retain the old references through commit. Reject preflight if this reserve is insufficient. Thus at default limits this lane permits at most **65,537** completion slots and **72 MiB** charged retained/pending metadata, apart from the separately bounded checkpoint/export and output buffers. With configured limits, the hard bounds are `max_completions+1` and `max_bytes+8 MiB`, with checked arithmetic. **The 8 MiB reserve cannot fund persistent growth above max_bytes**, and the extra slot does not enlarge the committed completion limit. Projected post-commit trim may free capacity for a watermark-bearing transaction, but not for purge before commit. Count, committed bytes, record size and active transient charge are independent checks; spare slots or spare transient bytes do not excuse excess committed bytes.

**Ordinary request pressure.** A new ordinary mutation that cannot fit returns RetryCapacity with untouched input/permit. The daemon parks it under bounded admission. It must not spin, poll unchanged charge or turn a capacity wait into NeedsAdmission merely because a permit exists. Wake and revalidate only after an actual relevant capacity change, such as committed watermark/terminal retirement, release of an ordinary reservation or completion of the active transient use. After grant release, fitting watermark-bearing mutations, independently initiated terminal close and safe fitting controls/jobs can pass pressure-blocked ordinary requests under the existing one-record rules. A read-only observed floor, checkpoint export/publication, cap release or timer is not durable retirement. None opens the Request gate during Control drain. This pressure bypass does not authorize a new request merely to unblock a protected control.

**R4: terminal protected-control capacity failure (coordinator-selected policy).** Preflight every valid non-request control that can grow charged state, including a protected dirty xattr CapUpdate, a cap-driven durable range addition, and a guarded lifecycle result/continuation. Internal resume work retains its control origin; `step(None, ...)` is not permission to reclassify it as an ordinary request. Check the projected committed charge against the base limits and the active copies against their separate hard bounds **before** changing metadata, ranges, cap sequences/rights, request/flush history, lifecycle guards/retirement or WAL state. Validate identity, flush deduplication and lifecycle guards without side effects first; an already-durable duplicate requires no new growth. Existing pending/unknown transactions retain their ordering and fail-closed reconciliation rules; do not speculate through them to calculate an admissible commit.

If the otherwise valid control's projected committed charge cannot fit, return **ControlCapacity** and make engine failure sticky as specified in section 3.3. A protected control that cannot fit the independent 8 MiB additional-copy allowance or the total active hard bound also has a terminal capacity outcome, not an indefinite wait. Capacity is neither corruption nor a protocol-identity error. It submits no record, changes no durable/canonical state and releases no effects or notices. In particular, it sends no FLUSH_ACK/grant, advances no cap sequence or flush watermark, releases no grant marker, retires no job/history and executes no fitting watermark Request held outside the engine. Do not ignore/truncate valid dirty xattrs or weaken a range/job guard to make the charge fit. Do not borrow persistent capacity from the transient reserve. Do not add a speculative persistent-headroom scheme for dirty rights.

**Synchronous daemon action.** In the same `Server::pump`/step-error handling turn, before an await, another engine turn, outbox/command dispatch or `drive_jobs`, latch the fatal capacity reason and stop admission, recovery readiness, client output and new engine/storage/data-job scheduling. Use the existing `fatal_error`/fail-stop path with a capacity reason, not its generic corruption or ordinary parked-input branch. Move the returned control into a bounded terminal-hold slot **before** cancellation/queue cleanup; reuse its already-admitted input charge. Return the unused permit to the adapter exactly once, but do not reuse it in that failed engine. Keep the unacknowledged returned control and other protected queued input owned until controlled shutdown. Fatal helpers must not clear it as if completed or erase pending-unknown operation identities needed for reconciliation. `ready_jobs`, `pending_jobs`, phase driving, output materialization and delayed completions must all honor the sticky failure; do not wait for a later Tick to set the fatal gate. Log the configured count/byte limits, the separate active bound, and the required charges (or explicit overflow), with the capacity classification. Do not log xattr contents, credentials or other client payloads; in particular, do not Debug-log the whole returning error/input. Its Display must also omit the payload.

Shutdown must not autonomously evict the sender, mark it Closing/Closed, run terminal cleanup, trim volatile/canonical history, discard a dirty control as drained, publish a checkpoint, release the marker or execute another Request to make space. It proves neither commit nor control-drain completion. Previously submitted uncertain operations remain subject to the existing fail-closed reconciliation; dropping a future is not cancellation or permission to release output. Preserve the exact known durable state and retry obligation. A replacement must keep pending jobs/receipts and reconstruct any still-unacknowledged flush from client retransmission, rather than treating shutdown as a synthetic acknowledgment.

This policy applies in **recovery and healthy serving**, including nonempty recovery with every key final and no missing-key cleanup that could free history. It deliberately gives up availability when a protected valid update cannot fit. The remedy is a reviewed startup with adequate larger limits on the **same StoreIdentity/filesystem/pools**, normal new-writer assignment/fencing and checked recovery. It is not reuse of a retired writer token, a live resize, an automatic restart loop or upgrade authorization. Changing configuration cannot revive the failed engine. The retransmitted control must still pass the independent record/transient bounds; increasing the base byte limit does not enlarge the fixed 8 MiB reserve. Metadata accounting does not bound retained file data or end-to-end messenger/RSS use.

**Startup-only limit configuration (R4 daemon interface).** Add `PersistenceConfig::retry_limits: engine::RetryLimits`. Parse these two ceph.conf keys through the existing `Config::from_args`/`CephConf::get` path; no new limit CLI option, environment override or dynamic resize worker is required:

Use current section precedence `[mds.<id>]` -> `[mds]` -> `[global]` -> default, and current case/space/dash/underscore key normalization (`crates/mds/src/config.rs::CephConf::get`). After trimming, require ASCII decimal digits fitting u64, checked conversion to usize, nonzero values and checked `max_completions+1` / `max_bytes+8*1024*1024`. ### 3.5 Exact persistent versions and absent-field semantics

The following is the **accepted E1 format contract**, not an implemented change:

| Structure | Old | New required encoding |
|---|---|---|
| `framing.rs` checksum/identity envelope | outer u16 version 1 | **Unchanged 1**, all FrameKind values unchanged. |
| `JournalRecord` semantic frame | v2/compat2 | **v3/compat3**; same field order, new nested completions. Frame selects `recovery_semantics`. |
| `CompletedRequest` nested frame | v1/compat1 | **v2/compat2**; append `reply_target_ino: Option<u64>` after `oldest_client_tid`, using existing u8 option tag and little-endian u64. |
| `EngineCheckpoint` semantic frame | v1/compat1 | **v2/compat2**; same field order; frame selects RetainedTargets3. |
| `CheckpointSession` nested frame | v1/compat1 | **v2/compat2**; same fields, durable floor/canonical-history semantics as section 3.4. |
| `rmds.authority` on format/WAL/sessions | leading byte 1 | **leading byte 2**, then required-semantics u16 **3**, then the existing revision/writer/predecessor fields and trailing CRC32C. All new bytes participate in CRC. |
| Original BootstrapPlan data, initial checkpoint objects, descriptor, rank session rows, inode/lifecycle/flush codecs and StoreIdentity | Current formats | **Unchanged**; preserve exact original bootstrap bytes and deterministic original objects. |

Authority v1 decodes as Legacy2; checkpoint v1 and WAL v2 decode as Legacy2. An absent `reply_target_ino` in completion v1 decodes as **unknown None**, not `created_ino`, zero or an inferred target. Do not run new retention/residency checks on valid legacy histories and call them corrupt. The clean-session check rejects any surviving legacy Open/Closing row/history with `StoreError::UpgradeRequired` (new variant), even if its target happens to be resident. There is no general legacy-history migration. In RetainedTargets3, the affected successful opcodes must have `Some(I)`; None is permitted only for the explicitly unaffected operations. `created_ino` and `reply_target_ino` equality is required for new-inode creators.

Decoders accept only the exact supported v/compat pairs above. Do not make `get_frame` skip a higher semantic version. Preserve `UnsupportedVersion` through store decode/error mapping; unknown required semantics/versions are not `Corrupt`. Ordinary semantics-3 loading requires checkpoint v2, nested session/completion v2 and uncovered WAL v3. The production WAL encoder writes only v3 and rejects a Legacy2 record; the upgrader never appends legacy records. Do not mix legacy WAL into an upgraded uncovered tail. The explicit upgrade loader alone can read legacy checkpoint v1/WAL v2 through its frozen tail. Schema 1 remains unsupported. A malformed or contradictory **semantics-3** target reference is Invalid/Corrupt; a valid older missing-target history is UpgradeRequired.

`BootstrapPlan::validate` currently reconstructs deterministic original objects using the current default checkpoint encoder. Freeze its legacy schema-2 encoder/decoder for these original plans. `inspect_format` must still decode/validate that original plan without regenerating a new root, reservation, object list or checksum from the semantics-3 encoder. Preserve bootstrap id, initial owner/range, initial allocator receipt and all immutable original bytes. Keep the existing Ready dependency checks. An unknown semantic version inside a plan must remain UnsupportedVersion, not the current blanket decode-to-Corrupt conversion. Separately authorized fresh setup writes an explicit **Legacy2 root-only seed**, not a semantics-3 image mislabeled as legacy. The process that completes this bootstrap must stop unready at the section 3.6 return boundary. It cannot upgrade its own three authorities in that startup. A subsequent monitor-assigned higher incarnation and different daemon GID, with explicit upgrade opt-in, must fence/acquire them and complete adoption before any OPEN/service. There is no fresh-only ownership exception and no authorization to create a fresh filesystem here.

### 3.7 Mechanically checked clean-session transition

Perform these steps only under a separately authorized upgrade execution assignment. The user deployment decision and this design do not provide it.

1. **Pause and fence.** Stop ordinary admission, OPEN/reconnect attachment, reply/flush acknowledgment, checkpoint scheduling and data workers. Drain all known current-process pending outcomes or refuse to start; there is no live in-place upgrade of an active serving engine. A process that just completed the fresh Legacy2 seed stops at section 3.6's U1 boundary; this step belongs to its eligible later assignment. Read actual format, WAL and rank-session authorities, including retained predecessor evidence and any persisted candidate producer. Confirm assigned rank 0 and beacon health. Fence the union of all other writers' exact addresses/nonces; wait for latest OSDMap and last-failure/predecessor epochs, verify entries and raise the barrier. Acquire **format, WAL and sessions independently**, each with its own identity/full-authority CAS and monotonic new ownership/revision. Preserve tail, published pointer, rows, allocator and next-incarnation counter. A legacy-to-new transition requires a different rank incarnation/gid from every **pre-transition legacy writer**; do not reuse an old handle's owner token. Do no engine mutation/service until all three are current. Recheck membership at every awaited boundary.
2. **Check the real source cut.** Freeze actual tail `T` and source descriptor `C`. Load the complete rank history under its own current authority **before** checked legacy restore/replay. Perform checksum/identity/sequence-checked checkpoint load and ordered legacy replay through **T**, not only through C.coverage. During replay, use the lifetime-bounded historical binding in section 3.6 U2 before each range-bearing image; preserve checkpoint sessions and let real terminal records retire the historical keys. Do not apply the final empty-engine-session condition to intermediate replay state. Initialize the load-only journal's committed position to that verified T before clean export; this initialization is not an append/flush/acknowledgment. Validate the complete materialized namespace, allocator provenance/reservations, stable identity, ranges, jobs and cleanup receipts. **After reaching T**, require zero Open and zero Closing rank rows, including rows with empty histories. Every Closed row must match a terminal engine receipt/sequence <=T; every receipt must have its matching Closed row. No engine session/request/flush history may remain. No orphan key, duplicate live incarnation, abandoned writer range or invalid job reference is allowed. Valid pending Purge/Truncate/RecoverSize jobs can remain only under the existing congruence rules and remain unexecuted during transition. A surviving legacy row/history returns UpgradeRequired, without deletion, synthetic close or automatic eviction. Genuine damage still returns its ordinary validation error.
3. **Write an immutable candidate.** Call `checkpoint_for_retry_upgrade`; compare all logical fields other than recovery semantics against the source image. Write a new materialized **EngineCheckpoint v2** plus manifest at generation C+1, coverage T, using the existing immutable naming/checksum scheme and unchanged outer/manifest formats. The session vector must be empty; retain every inode, allocator state, job, counter and cleanup receipt. Read the complete dependency chain back and validate it. Do not publish it yet. Capacity/generation pressure stops the transition; never delete old checkpoints/WAL or change identity to make space. A crash here leaves only an unreferenced candidate, not an adopted filesystem.
4. **Install the irreversible format barrier.** Verify the upgrade value is absent in the stable format snapshot. In one operation on **rmds.format**, compare identity, current full authority and Ready phase, then set authority v2/required semantics 3 with revision+1, phase UpgradingRetryTargets and the exact upgrade plan/id/T/C/target/producer. Every legal format mutation changes the guarded authority revision; this guards the inspected absence. Do not assume CMPXATTR treats a missing xattr as an empty value. Preserve original plan data. This is the first durable required-version change. All newer loaders see an incomplete transition; old loaders reject the authority version. No rollback to Legacy2 is supported after this point. Re-read and reconcile this operation before any dependent promotion.
5. **Promote sessions independently.** Re-read/load rank rows and repeat the all-Closed/receipt check. Compare **rmds.sessions.0** identity, full own authority and next-incarnation bytes in one operation. Set its authority v2/required semantics 3 and revision+1. Do not modify rows, cleanup_seq, client indexes or incarnation allocation. This is not protected by the format/WAL CAS.
6. **Promote WAL and publish atomically on that object only.** Verify candidate bytes again and recheck T/C and current WAL authority. One compound operation on **rmds.wal.0.0** compares identity, owner, full own authority, tail T, exact source descriptor C and trim position. It sets authority v2/required semantics 3 with revision+1 **and** the target checkpoint pointer. Preserve tail, batch identity and remaining keys. This same-object operation prevents a semantics-3 control pointing at a legacy checkpoint (or the reverse). A replacement may publish the exact persisted immutable candidate made by its fenced predecessor; only this upgrade path relaxes normal publication's current-epoch-name check. Validate its original framed owner/name, identity, coverage and checksum; never rename/rewrite the candidate under the replacement owner. The candidate uses the existing zero checkpoint transaction field; the guarded intent binds it to the upgrade id through its exact descriptor. Normal checkpoint publication retains its existing owner-name rule.
7. **Trim and finish.** Trim covered WAL keys through T in bounded operations under the new WAL's own identity/owner/full-authority/descriptor/trim guards. Keep immutable older checkpoints and the original bootstrap plan. Re-read all three authorities, candidate state, pointer, tail/trim T and rank rows; repeat the clean join and logical equality checks. Then CAS **rmds.format only**, comparing current full authority, Upgrading phase and exact plan/id, to Ready with v2/required semantics 3 and revision+1. Keep the origin plan. Only this checked Ready3 permits subsequent ordinary load. No mutation or acknowledgment may precede it. New sessions/records start only after the normal checked load/E2/monitor service gates.

Every semantics-3 takeover preserves required semantics. Every new WAL append, publication and trim must compare its own **full encoded authority**, in addition to existing identity/owner/tail/descriptor guards; migrate the current owner-only `guards` helper accordingly. Session mutations already compare their own full authority/counter and must encode the same required semantics on every revision. Neither an upgraded format object nor a WAL guard authorizes a session-object write. Allocator/shardmap identities and reservation data stay unchanged; they grant no retry/service authority.

### 3.8 Crash, unknown-outcome and downgrade proof

| Cut | Required recovery |
|---|---|
| Fresh frozen Legacy2 bootstrap reaches Ready | Its completing process A returns UpgradeRequired before Loaded and stops unready, with no upgrade intent/candidate. Only a later eligible monitor-assigned process B with explicit opt-in fences/acquires and upgrades the unchanged seed. No same-owner acquisition or self-blocklist. |
| Before the format barrier | Source pointer/Legacy2 remains authoritative. Candidate objects are unreferenced. A later opt-in rechecks the actual source cut; do not treat an orphan as upgrade evidence. |
| After format barrier; sessions/WAL still Legacy2 | Old startup stops at format Authority v2. New startup stays unready and resumes the persisted plan. It may read legacy source state only in this upgrade mode, loading guarded rank history first and using the section 3.6 U2 bindings during replay through T. |
| After session promotion; before WAL promotion | Preserve the upgraded session authority/rows/counter. Independently fence/acquire the actual mixed authorities. Recheck the source with historical bindings through T, then all-Closed/receipt/empty-history state, and publish only the persisted candidate. |
| Unknown WAL promotion result | Read authority, tail, pointer, batch and trim together. Exact promoted authority+target pointer proves the step; exact unchanged source permits identical guarded retry. A half-promoted same-object result is invalid, not permission to reset either field. |
| After publication or during trim; format still Upgrading | Load the persisted candidate at T; it includes all data/jobs/receipts and no history. Resume bounded trim, checks and final format CAS. Do not require already-trimmed legacy records to reconstruct it. |
| Unknown final Ready result | Exact desired Ready3 authority/revision plus matching origin plan proves completion for that owner. Otherwise unchanged Upgrading permits identical CAS after checks. A different writer requires reinspection/fencing. Do not acknowledge/service from an uncertain result. |
| Replacement at any stage | Reinspect actual current writers/semantics on all three objects and the recorded producer. Fence/acquire each independently, preserving versions/intent/counters. A changed authority stops the old attempt. Candidate producer ownership is historical; it is not current write authority. |
| Ready3 ordinary restart or attempted old binary | New loader requires all three guards/loaded semantic versions to agree, then normal checked join/E2. Old format/WAL/session Authority v1 decoders reject v2 even when WAL is empty and the checkpoint has no completions. No old writer can reopen and later acknowledge new-format state. |

Unknown mutations freeze transition progress. Reconcile exact intended bytes/state under the same logical id. Retry only against the exact unchanged expected snapshot; any other writer requires reinspection/fencing, not a new sequence, descriptor or fabricated success. Format inspection must read its plan, phase, authority and upgrade value from one guarded/atomic snapshot, or verify an unchanged authority revision around those reads; today's separate unguarded body/xattr reads are insufficient for this transition. Missing required intent/dependencies, conflicting candidate bytes or impossible version/phase combinations fail closed. Do not guess completion from a timeout.

## 4. D6: active timers and reconnect deadline

### Healthy ACTIVE service

Use monotonic runtime clocks, not persisted timestamps. Let `S = max(1, MDSMap.session_timeout)` seconds and `A = max(1, MDSMap.session_autoclose)` seconds. The map supplies both; defaults are **60 s** and **300 s** (`/home/ubuntu/ceph/src/mds/MDSMap.h:605–606`). For an Open session, measure idle time from the later of last valid RENEWCAPS and last valid authenticated session traffic. Initialize activity at durable OPEN/final reconnect. Account for received eligible traffic before evaluating expiry. Do not let unknown, rejected or old-generation packets renew activity.

Apply these checks in order, using `>=` at each threshold:

1. Before `S`, retain the Open session.
2. At or after `S`, if idle time reached `A`, start fenced eviction, before checking metadata.
3. Without a `timeout` key, enter ordinary STALE at `S`. A valid RENEWCAPS resumes it. An already Stale session reaches autoclose at `A` measured from its last RENEWCAPS, **not from STALE entry**. Stale handling must still fence before dropping unsafe writer rights.
4. With a `timeout` key, do **not** enter ordinary STALE or invalidate retained caps/locks. After trimming whitespace, accept ASCII decimal digits `[0-9]+` that fit `u32` seconds. Zero means no per-session early expiry, not immunity from autoclose. Malformed/empty/signed/overflow values retain timeout-session treatment, act as zero, and produce a diagnostic. Preserve the stored text. A positive `P` starts fenced eviction once idle time also reaches `P`.

Thus a silent timeout session expires at `max(S, min(A, P))` for positive `P`, or `max(S, A)` for zero/malformed. At S=60/A=300, P=30 expires at 60, P=120 at 120, and P=0/300/600 at 300 seconds. An ordinary silent session becomes Stale at 60 and reaches autoclose at 300, **not 360**. Reclaim or explicit eviction can end retention earlier, always through fencing and durable close.

Run these timers only in healthy confirmed ACTIVE service. Beacon/owner failure cannot authorize rights changes; preserve the existing lag/admission safety gates. These timers never complete a reconnect gather. The engine remains clock-free.

This resolves the shorthand in minimum-feature-set section 4.2: timeout sessions retain rights until reclaim, explicit eviction, positive override expiry or map autoclose. `mds/Server.cc::find_idle_sessions` (1211–1353) checks ordinary timeout first, autoclose before the override, zero as no override expiry, and positive override as direct eviction without STALE. The strict decimal/malformed rule above is explicit Rust policy; do not copy `strtoul`'s partial/base-0 parsing.

### Bounded reconnect window and retention

`MDSMap` has no reconnect-timeout field. Read the existing `mds_reconnect_timeout` setting through daemon config precedence; default **R=45 s**. Require a positive finite duration with representable `2*R` and monotonic deadline arithmetic; reject invalid configuration before service. Snapshot R for the attempt. This matches `common/options/mds.yaml.in` (436–444). Do not derive R from S, A or client `timeout`.

Start `t0` only when the monitor confirms RECONNECT. Let L start at t0. Update L monotonically for validated current-attachment input from a still-missing gather member: reconnect chunks, eligible early retries/cap controls and renewals. Set `L = max(L, t0, receive_time)` for eligible input held across map confirmation or delayed attachment. Do not extend on malformed/unknown/old-generation traffic or completed-member duplicate finals. Check the current deadline before accepting later input; expiry freezes the missing keys and cannot be reversed by a late packet.

```text
deadline = min(t0 + 2*R, max(t0 + R, L + R/2))
```

At or after the current deadline, stop accepting new reconstruction for missing keys and begin their fenced durable eviction. With defaults, the base interval is **45 s**, the activity interval is **22.5 s**, and the hard limit is **90 s**. Traffic cannot reset t0 or extend past 90 s. All-final/all-Closed gathers may finish earlier. Fencing/storage failures may delay safe completion beyond the input deadline; never release rights just because time elapsed. No extra timeout-session/Ganesha reclaim hold follows this window. C++ `Server::reconnect_tick` (1870–1889) supplies the base/half-interval behavior; the 2R hard limit is the bounded Rust policy.

Retain the following state under the existing bounded recovery budget:

- v3 completes once. v5 has no chunk index; `more=false`, including an empty final, completes once. Validate each whole chunk before installation. Replacement inode entries replace caps/locks, not append them. A duplicate final from the completed attachment is a no-op: no reinstallation, gather change or second OPEN.
- Before final, a matching connection Reset/Closed calls `reset_reconnect(key)` for that incomplete generation only. Retain durable identity, retry/flush histories, missing membership, address union and protected early dirty controls. Revalidate retained controls against replacement reconstruction; if they cannot be safely applied, fence/close the key. Do not count their deletion as drain.
- After final, transport loss retains reconstructed rights, locks and completed membership. A same-authenticated-key replacement may attach only after durable address/authorization update. It resumes the retained funnel/sequence and control state; it cannot install a second reconnect stream in this attempt. If it needs new reconstruction, require fenced durable close instead. Do not discard final state on a transport event alone.
- Freeze a key against attachment when eviction starts. Retain its full persisted address/nonce union, controls and rights until verified fencing and Closing → committed cleanup → Closed finish. Late/stale completions cannot attach or close another generation. A changed address union invalidates an incomplete fence proof.
- Charge raw input, applied caps/locks/realms, corrections, retained controls and attachment state. Freeing raw bytes does not free the installed-state charge. A replacement or timer extension resets neither the aggregate budget nor t0. Overflow remains fail-closed.
